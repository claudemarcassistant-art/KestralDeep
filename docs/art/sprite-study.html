kestrel deep · art direction

Sprite study

A proposal for redrawing the player and the creatures. Everything here is drawn the way the game draws: canvas code at the game's real pixel size (12 px tiles), then enlarged without smoothing. So what you see is what would ship, and the drawing code on this page could be handed to Claude Code as a reference.

01 · in context

On a deck

sprites lighting sprite outlines player

Shown at the game's real size (one screen is 512 x 288; this is a quarter of it). On a dark deck, creatures catch light on the side facing your lamp, and the parts that glow on their own (the spitter's sac, the ghost, spider eyes, the guard's flashlight) carry through the dark, so you read them by their light before you see their shape.

At game pixels a person is about 10 pixels across, so a lot of the detail can only be suggested. Double shows the same drawing code on a canvas with twice the pixels (the world and tile size stay the same, only the sharpness changes). The game could switch to that; it costs some drawing time on Large decks and would need checking.

02 · the player

Seen from above

Bird's-eye view of a person: head and shoulders from straight above, feet stepping out from under the body, arms that actually hold the weapon. Take A, the survey tech, is the player. Takes B and C stay on file as the start of possible starter archetypes or crew. People stay clean-edged; creatures are painted, so you never lose yourself in a crowd.

03 · creatures

Painted, not outlined

Each creature is a cluster of small dabs in four tones of its own colour, lit from one side, with no black outline. Personality comes from colour temperature and motion: the husk sways and drags a foot, the crawler moves in bursts and smears, the spitter's sac swells and glows before it fires.

04 · crew

The people still down here

The current NPCs, rebuilt from the same parts as the player: same body, same arms and head, with their own clothes, headgear and the props of their trade. Each has a small idle activity so you can tell what they offer from across the room. Because they share the player's parts, any of them could later become a starter archetype.

Today they are drawn standing up, seen from the front, which is a different camera from everything else in the game. These match the top-down view.

05 · the deck itself

Floors, walls and an anomaly

The v0.78 tiles are the standard floor plates, corridor grates and walls from the version I have here (they are unchanged since v0.41). The proposed tiles add depth (soft shading where floor meets wall, lit wall tops) and give each biome its own kit: a mix of floor styles (each room picks one), a corridor style, a mix of wall styles, and a style of room light with its own colour, pool shape and steadiness. The three rooms here show the biome's three most likely floors. The kits share styles (tread plate turns up in three biomes, bulkheads in four), so biomes feel related but distinct.

The anomaly sits in the lower room; with v0.78 tiles it is drawn the v0.78 way, with proposed tiles in the variant you pick. If the tiles in the current build look different from the v0.78 ones here, they changed after v0.78; tell me which look you prefer and I will match it.

kept · all other decks

Anomaly, unstable

The core is never quite round and its size wanders on several rhythms that never line up. It hiccups at uneven gaps: sometimes two or three close together, sometimes nothing for several seconds. Each swell is a different size, and only the big ones flash and send out a ring. The rim is a broken ring of painted dabs, and now and then the picture around it tears sideways for a frame.

kept · electrical decks

Anomaly, crackling

No big swells. It discharges in stuttering bursts of short, forked arcs from the rim that throw sparks, its rim flickers with each burst, and the core jitters. Between bursts, the odd single snap. Used on electrical decks.

06 · liquids

Water and other spills

Left: a pool going from puddle edges to swimming depth. Top middle to right: ice over shallow water, an oil slick, a toxic spill. Bottom: a slug's slime trail and molten slag with a rim of cooled slag. Today, water is coloured squares stacked by depth. Proposed, water is a smooth depth field, so shores follow the depth instead of the tile grid, with a wet line at the edge, light rippling in the shallows, slow swells in the deep and drifting glints. Each biome tints its water (greener in Brine Works, colder in Cold Store). The other spills get their own surface and motion.

07 · armaments

In hand and on the floor

Each armament in the survey tech's hands, using it, next to today's version. Today most weapons are a short dark line from a round body. Proposed, each has its own silhouette and colour, sits in the hand that holds it, and shows how it works: the sidearm kicks back, the scattergun pumps, the bow draws, the spear jabs, the whip lashes, the chainsaw shakes and spits sparks. The third picture is the armament lying on the floor, so each one can be told apart as a pickup instead of sharing one generic weapon icon. The industrial blower and the stun gun from Batch 3 are included so they are designed before they are built.

08 · loot

Items on the floor

Each pair shows the v0.78 item on the left and the proposed one on the right. Proposed items keep their size but are drawn as small objects lit from the top left, and each sits on a soft pool of its category's colour, so you can tell ammo from medicine from a key at a glance, even in a dark corner. A shine sweeps across them now and then instead of a single blinking pixel.

09 · interactables

Things you can use

v0.78 on the left, proposed on the right. Same footprint and same shapes, with shading from the top left, a lit top edge, a soft shadow on the floor, and a halo around anything that gives off light (screens, signs, status lights, chest locks), so working machines read from across a dark room.

Crates arrived in v0.82, after the version I have here, so the "v0.78" crate is a stand-in. If something you use a lot is missing, tell me and I will add it.

10 · if you like it

Rules for the style

four tones eachEvery creature gets a dark-to-light ramp of four colours in its own hue. No shared outline colour; the dark tone is the edge.
one light sideTones are picked by which side faces the light: overhead fixtures on lit decks, your lamp on dark ones.
motion is personalitySway, burst, swell, bob, twitch. Each creature has one signature movement that also warns what it does.
glow tells dangerParts that glow on their own are the dangerous parts: a sac about to spit, eyes in a duct, a guard's beam.
people stay crispThe player, crew and guards are clean shapes with a two-tone bevel, built from one shared body, so people always read against painted creatures.
biomes have kitsEach biome mixes a few shared floor, corridor and wall styles in its own proportions and has its own kind of room light, so biomes feel related but distinct.
outlines as an optionAn Options setting draws a one-pixel outline around every sprite in a brightened version of its own main colour (not black), for players who want things to stand out more.
same cost to drawUnder 100 small rectangles per creature, no images. Dab layouts are made once per type and reused.

Take A is chosen. To send it on, say which creatures and crew to keep, change or drop. It would become an "art pass" request: the drawing code on this page goes in as the reference, and the remaining creatures and bosses follow the same rules.
